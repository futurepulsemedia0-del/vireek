## 2. Data export method

Tenant data stays in the tenant Google account (export via the tenant account, Takeout [VERIFY]). Vireek holds only encrypted OAuth tokens. Ads and Business Profile reports can be exported through their APIs [VERIFY].

## 3. Alternatives

Calendar: Microsoft/CalDAV. Maps: Mapbox, HERE, or OSM stack. Warehouse: Snowflake or Postgres adapters (already in `data-platform/adapters`). Push: direct FCM-free native push or OneSignal.

## 4. Estimated switch effort

**L** — ≈ 6–10 person-weeks across all services; individually S–M [VERIFY]. Scale: S < 2 person-weeks · M 2–5 · L 5–10 · XL > 10 (one engineer, including tests and rollout; excludes vendor lead times, contract negotiation and tenant coordination). The measured coupling tier above is an input, not the estimate: it counts import reachability, not difficulty.

## 5. Exit steps

1. Declare the trigger (vendor insolvency, price/terms change, security incident, regulatory block, quality drop) and record the decision and date.
2. Freeze new dependency: feature-flag new onboarding to the vendor.
3. Run the export in section 2 and verify row counts and checksums against Vireek tables; store the export in a private bucket.
4. Build or enable the replacement behind the seam in section 1; shadow-run before any traffic moves.
5. Cut over progressively; keep rollback available until the vendor contract ends.
6. Revoke vendor credentials, request deletion of vendor-held data and written confirmation [COUNSEL], and update the public sub-processor list and customer notice (`scripts/compliance/gen-subprocessors-by-region.mjs --check`) [COUNSEL].
7. Provider-specific: Handle each service as its own sub-exit; do not attempt one big switch.
8. Provider-specific: Google OAuth app verification must be re-done for a replacement Cloud project [VERIFY].
