Vireek — logo concepts

Three wordmark concepts, original geometric letterforms (no typeface), outlined. Each is shown on light and dark, then at 96/64/32/24/16 px for the symbol and 48/32/24/16 px (height) for the wordmark.

Concept A — Pulse tittle Recommended · shipped

The i-dot becomes a separate orange circle — the “call coming in” pulse. The symbol puts the same dot inside the V, so wordmark and monogram share one idea.

Distinctiveness●●●○○
Legibility @16px●●●●●
Trademark-ability●●●○○
System fit (symbol, loader, favicon)●●●●●

Strongest at small sizes and the only one that scales cleanly into a favicon, loader and avatar. Weakness: a coloured i-dot is a common device; ownability comes from dot + V together, not the dot alone.

Concept B — Voice bars

Both e-crossbars become orange bars — two level-meter bars sitting in the word. In the symbol, two bars of unequal height sit in the V.

Distinctiveness●●●●○
Legibility @16px●●●○○

Most ownable idea, but below ~24px the orange bars merge into a strike-through and the e’s lose their eye. The two-bar symbol is busy at 16px.

Concept C — Uptick V

The right arm of the V rises past the cap line and its tip turns orange — recovered revenue.

Reads as a check-mark/“broken V” and the orange chip looks detached. Risk of resembling existing V-tick marks. Not recommended.

Typographic test — Title case vs lowercase

Concept A in both cases. Title case is shipped: the capital V is the same V as the monogram, and the dot sits tight against the i stem; lowercase “v” is too small to anchor the word and the dot floats.

Recommendation: Concept A

It is the only concept that stays legible at 16 px, and its single idea (a pulse dot) transfers to the monogram, the animated loader and the favicon without redrawing anything. See the honest self-review in the delivery notes: the dot alone is not trademark-strong, so a professional should refine the V and the dot relationship before filing.
